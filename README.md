## Background
Agriculture in Europe has undergone significant transformations over the last 200 years, deeply influencing and being influenced by the environment.
In the 19th century, European agriculture was largely traditional, relying on small-scale farming, crop rotation, and natural fertilizers. The Industrial Revolution brought mechanization, increasing productivity but also leading to deforestation and soil depletion. The 20th century saw major advancements with the Green Revolution, introducing synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, and high-yield crops. While this boosted food production, it also caused environmental issues such as soil degradation, water pollution, and biodiversity loss.
In recent decades, concerns over climate change and sustainability have led to a push for more environmentally friendly farming practices. The European Union has promoted organic farming, crop diversification, and agroforestry to reduce the sector's ecological footprint. Despite these efforts, challenges remain, including greenhouse gas emissions from livestock and land-use changes. Balancing food security with environmental sustainability continues to be a key issue for European agriculture today.

## Objectives
In this work package we would first plan to make an inventory of existing national agricultural statistics. National Statistical Institutes (NSIs) in the EU report a wide range of agricultural statistics to Eurostat, but the most basic and consistently reported agricultural statistics include the following core domains:

•	Crop Production Statistics
•	Livestock Statistics
•	Agricultural Price Statistics
•	Economic Accounts for Agriculture (EAA)
•	Farm Structure Survey (FSS)
•	Pesticide and Fertilizer Use Statistics
T
hese are standardized under various EU regulations to ensure comparability across Member States. Most data are publicly available via Eurostat's agriculture database .
Furthermore, we plan to do a feasibility study when it comes to long time series, 100 years or more, and breaking figures down to LAU level. Some possible sources are:

•	Aslak Bolt's Cadastre (1432–1433)
•	Agricultural Censuses (1825, 1907-)
•	National Archives of Norway 
•	NTNU University Library Special Collections 
•	Norwegian Historical Data Centre (NHDC) 

Once sources and availability of data have been checked out, we plan to investigate possibilities of using newly remastered geodata for LAU.

## Expected outcomes and deliveries
Expected outcomes are inventories of existing agricultural statistics at different geographical levels and likewise inventories of available sources of statistics with quality assessments. These outcomes will be parts of deliveries of this work package. Where relevant, outcomes from this WP be included into deliveries in WP2.
